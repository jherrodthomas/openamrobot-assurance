# OpenAMRobot assurance, explained

[OpenAMRobot](https://github.com/openAMRobot) is an open mobile manipulator platform by Botshare LTD: a ROS 2
navigation stack on a Raspberry Pi, a small firmware layer on a Teensy that is the last thing before the motors,
and an open hardware record. This project forks it and adds what a machine needs before it may work near people
and what upstream, by its own account, does not have: a risk assessment, safety functions with a required
Performance Level, a hardwired safety chain, an electrical installation that can be switched off, a cybersecurity
case, and a safety case that publishes its own counter-evidence. The standards are the machinery set: ISO 12100,
ISO 3691-4, ISO 13849-1, EN 60204-1, IEC 62443, ISO/IEC TR 5469 and the Machinery Regulation.

This wiki is the orientation layer. It explains how the project is organised, how the work flows through its
phases and gates, which standard shapes which part of the record, and where to look. It never states a safety
claim: those live in the [assurance record](../assurance/README.md), where they are reviewed.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **OpenAMRobot is research hardware and this record is a draft prepared for engineering review.** Nothing has
> passed verification review, confirmation review or a functional safety assessment. The robot is not CE-marked,
> no safety function reaches any Performance Level today, and no configuration is approved for operation around
> people.

## How the work flows: one lifecycle, seven gates

![The integrated lifecycle as a grid: the seven phases P0 to P6 across the top, each closed by its gate; the eight discipline lanes down the side, with the number of activities each lane performs in each phase; the confirmation measures and the cross-domain interfaces per gate below; a dashed feedback arc from operation back into the concept phase.](../docs/assets/openamrobot/process/lifecycle-swimlanes.svg)

*Every discipline works inside the same phase and decides at the same gate; there is no separate cybersecurity or
machine-learning lifecycle. Drawn from the process model by
[`process_figures.py`](../assurance/process/tools/process_figures.py). [Processes](Processes.md) explains the
phases, hold points and review levels; [Process maps](Process-maps.md) shows the swimlane of every phase.*

## The safety argument in one picture

![Platform architecture in three layers: the inherited ROS 2 stack, hardened as defence in depth; the Teensy firmware as the last software before the motors; and a hardwired safety chain as the checker of last resort, the only layer with a Performance Level target. A cross-cutting assurance column lists the record that argues it.](../docs/assets/openamrobot/architecture.svg)

*The ROS 2 stack proposes, the firmware checks, and a hardwired chain that nothing in software can override is the
checker of last resort. Only the chain carries a PL target. The argument is
[WP-M-01 §6](../assurance/01-management/WP-M-01-assurance-strategy.md#6-safety-architecture-argument-the-central-strategy);
[Reading the safety case](Reading-the-safety-case.md) explains how the claims, evidence and defeaters are recorded.*

## Where the record stands today

![Record status card generated from the records: gates passed, work products by status and by gate, gap findings by severity and area, decisions, interfaces, confirmation measures and the hold point.](../docs/assets/openamrobot/status.svg)

*Generated from the records on every pull request; no number on it is typed by hand. The
[Living Safety Case dashboard](../assurance/case/README.md) shows the claims, evidence and defeaters the same way,
and [Roadmap and status](Roadmap-and-status.md) lists what is drafted, skeleton and planned.*

> Assurance status represents recorded engineering evidence and review state. It does not constitute
> certification or approval for operation around people.

## What this wiki is not

- **Not a work product.** Nothing here carries a document-control header or counts as evidence.
- **Not the place for hazard, requirement or safety-function text.** Each page links to the record that owns it.
- **Not illustrated by hand.** Every image is a figure generated from the repository's records, or rendered from
  them at sync time.
- **Not edited in place.** Pages are kept in the repository under [`wiki/`](../wiki/README.md), reviewed by pull
  request, and mirrored here on merge. An edit made on the wiki itself is overwritten by the next sync.
